# demo-reel — 1 · Goal

*Source: Dinis voice memo, 1 Sep 2026. Version 0.2.93. Status: **PROPOSED —
nothing in this pack exists yet** except where explicitly marked as shipped.*

## The problem, in the words it arrived in

> "SGit AI has published a number of vaults. I basically need to have a video for
> each one that describes the capabilities, the walkthroughs — because the whole
> thing is fundamentally much more visual. We already have good descriptions on
> the website, but it needs to be a lot more dynamic."

The gap is not documentation. The gap is that a semantic graph, an encrypted
vault, a fractal panel layout — the things this project actually builds — are
**spatial and temporal**, and a paragraph is neither. Every feature that ships
deserves a ninety-second video, and today making one costs an afternoon of
screen-recording and editing that nobody has.

## What we are building

A tool that turns **an ordered list of annotated screenshots plus narration text**
into a finished video, in both **landscape (16:9)** and **shorts (9:16)** format,
authored **by an agent** or by hand.

The unit is a **scene**: three to five screenshots that belong together, one block
of narration spoken over all of them, and on-screen text that carries the same
point for the majority of viewers who will watch it **with the sound off**.

## Why this is an extension, not a new tool

`narrated-review` already produces exactly the raw material. Its unit is a
capture: *a screenshot, the words spoken about it, and the alignment between
them* — which is a scene missing only its annotations and its second aspect
ratio. A `session.json` from a narrated walkthrough should import as a first cut
of a reel, and often that first cut will be most of the work.

And `video-creator` (**shipped**, v0.1.47) already does the back half: slide
images plus per-slide narration text → Kokoro TTS audio → canvas + MediaRecorder
→ a WebM file. **The pipeline this brief describes is roughly 60% built.**

So this pack is deliberately not "build a video editor". It is:

1. an **annotation layer** that is data rather than pixels,
2. a **scene document** an agent can write,
3. **two aspect ratios from one document**,
4. an **on-screen text track**, because muted is the default,
5. and the **import from narrated-review** that connects the two ends.

## What does not exist yet — every item below is PROPOSED

- Any annotation primitive: callout, arrow, circle, box, highlight, **blur**, tagline.
- Any notion of a **scene** holding more than one image under one narration block.
  `video-creator` is strictly one slide ↔ one narration ↔ one audio duration.
- Any **vertical** render. `video-creator` is fixed at 1280×720 and letterboxes
  to fit.
- Any **on-screen text** rendering. Nothing draws captions today.
- Any import from `narrated-review` into `video-creator`.
- Any deterministic frame clock — see the pacing risk in part 3.
- Any **video clip as a shot**. Everything today is a still; see Decision 9.

## Success looks like

A person or an agent publishes a vault, runs one command or drops one
`session.json`, adjusts a few callouts, and gets two files: a 90-second landscape
video for the website and YouTube, and a 45-second vertical cut for social. Both
readable with the sound off. Total human time: **under ten minutes.**

## Explicit non-goals

- **No word-level audio/text synchronisation.** The memo rules it out for now, and
  it is the difference between a weekend and a month. Captions are authored per
  shot, with their own timing.
- **No general video editor.** No trimming source footage, no transitions library,
  no multi-track audio. `sg-video-editor` exists for editing; this generates.
- **No talking heads, no webcam, no music bed** in v1.
- **No server-side rendering.** Everything stays in the browser, on the user's
  machine, consistent with every other tool here.

## The one number that matters

Time from "the feature is shipped" to "the video is published". Today that is a
half-day and therefore usually never. The target is **ten minutes**, which is the
threshold at which it actually happens every time.
