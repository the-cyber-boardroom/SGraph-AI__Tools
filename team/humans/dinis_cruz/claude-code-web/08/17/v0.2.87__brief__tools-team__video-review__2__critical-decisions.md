# Brief — Video Review · 2/5 Critical Decisions

Eight decisions. Two of them (3 and 4) are the whole tool; the rest are mostly
"reuse what already works, and say so". **PROPOSED — does not exist yet** unless
a pin is quoted.

> **AMENDED 17 Aug 2026 — read part 1 §0 first.** This capability shipped as an
> INGEST MODE inside `narrated-review` (v0.1.4), not as a separate tool. The
> decisions about timestamps, frame choice and grouping are in the shipped code;
> the tool boundary, the `core/sg-review-pairs` extraction and the separate
> manifest/registry entry are NOT, and are no longer planned for v1.

## Decision 1 — Timestamps come from cutting the audio, not from asking a model

The requirement is "transcribe the audio **with timestamps**". There are three
ways to get them and they are not equally trustworthy.

| Option | Mechanism | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A. Ask a chat model to emit timings | "return JSON with start/end per sentence" | **Rejected.** Models invent plausible timings. A timestamp that is confidently wrong is worse than none, because every frame downstream is chosen from it. |
| **B. Cut first, then transcribe** | Decode to PCM → `core/sg-live-capture` `createVad` cuts at sustained pauses → transcribe each clip separately | **Recommended.** The bounds are *structural*: we know each clip's start and end because we made the cut. The model is only asked to do the thing it is good at — turn this audio into these words. Same engine `narrated-review` uses, pointed at a file instead of a microphone. |
| C. Dedicated STT with word timings | OpenRouter `/audio/transcriptions` verbose_json | Not available: `core/sg-transcribe`'s `audio-models.js` lists the two STT entries as `available: false` ("STT — coming soon"). Do not present a gated path as working. |
| **D. Local Whisper, word-level** | `core/sg-whisper` `transcribeBuffer(model, audio, { timestamps: true })` → `chunks` | **Yes, as an opt-in second lane.** It exists today, returns word/segment chunks, costs nothing and needs no key — at the price of a ~74 MB model download and slower processing. Right for "I need the exact word", wrong as the default. |

**The trade to say out loud:** B gives *utterance-level* timestamps (typically a
sentence or two), not word-level. That is the correct granularity for this tool —
a pair is a moment, not a word — and D is there when it isn't.

## Decision 2 — Audio out of the container: reuse the publisher's ladder

`video-publisher` already solved this and the routes are code-verified. Reuse
`api/audio-router.js`'s shape rather than re-deriving it:

| Route | When | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1. FFmpeg stream-copy remux → `.m4a` | the usual case for mp4/mov | one FFmpeg load (~30 MB from CDN, once) |
| 2. `core/sg-audio-decode` `blobToWav` | when remux fails, or the file is small | no FFmpeg |
| 3. Fail with a typed `no-audio-stream` | a silent video | — |

Note this tool never needs route 1 of the *publisher's* list (a separate audio
stream from the recorder) because the video arrives as a finished file.

## Decision 3 — The picture LEADS the words (this tool's practical catch)

The sibling tool has a catch — people speak before they press, so audio must be
continuous. **This tool has the mirror-image catch, and it is not symmetric.**

In a screencast the grammar is: **change the screen, then talk about it.** The
visual event precedes the sentence about it. So the naive choice — "take a frame
at the midpoint of the segment" — is wrong in two different ways:

- If the presenter navigated just before speaking, the midpoint frame is right,
  but only by luck; a slow transition, animation or page load puts the midpoint
  mid-render and yields a blurred, half-drawn frame.
- If the presenter says *"and now if I click here…"* the relevant change happens
  **during or after** the sentence, so the midpoint shows the old screen.

Both happen in the same recording. The design must therefore search a window
that starts a little **before** the segment and ends a little **after** it:

```
     segment N speech           segment N+1
  ├──────────────────────┤   ├───────────────
  │                      │   │
──┴──────────────────────┴───┴───────────────►  t
 ↑                          ↑
 leadMs (default 2500)      lagMs (default 1200)
 └──────── frame search window ────────┘
```

## Decision 4 — Choose the frame by stability, keep the alternates

Within that window, sample frames on a grid (default every 500 ms) and score them.

| Option | Mechanism | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A. Midpoint frame | one seek | Rejected as the default (Decision 3), kept as the last-resort fallback |
| **B. Settle detection** | downscale each candidate to a small greyscale thumbnail, take the mean absolute difference between consecutive ones, and prefer a frame that sits in the **longest run of low change** — the screen that was *held* while it was being discussed | **Recommended default.** Pure local computation, no model cost, and it encodes the actual grammar of a screencast: the thing being talked about is the thing left on screen. |
| C. Model picks | send N candidates + the segment text to a vision model | **Opt-in refine, not the default.** It is the same multimodal call `narrated-review` already makes for cleanup, so it costs nothing to build — but it multiplies image spend, and B is right most of the time. Offer it as "improve this pair" per pair and "refine all" for the whole session. |
| D. Text-anchored (OCR) | match on-screen words to the transcript | Out of scope. Interesting, expensive, and B usually gets there first. |

**Keep every candidate on the pair.** They are already decoded; discarding them
means recomputation the moment the user disagrees. The review pass then offers a
filmstrip and switching frames is instant. This is the direct answer to "we will
still need to do a couple of tweaks" — the most common tweak is *this frame is
the wrong one*, and it should cost one click, not a re-run.

## Decision 5 — Group adjacent segments that share a frame

A person explaining one screen for thirty seconds produces four or five VAD
utterances. Four pairs of the same screenshot is not a document, it is a stutter.

**Merge adjacent segments whose chosen frames are near-identical** (the same
cheap difference metric as Decision 4, above a similarity threshold), and
concatenate their text. The merge happens *after* transcription and *before* the
document, and is reversible in the review pass ("split here"), because the
transcript segments are retained underneath.

This is also the main cost lever on long videos: it collapses the pair count
towards the number of *distinct screens*, which is the number a reader actually
wants.

## Decision 6 — What is shared, and how (the real architecture decision)

The back half of `narrated-review` is not "similar" to what this tool needs — it
is *the same*. Copying it forks it.

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Copy the files into a new tool | **Rejected.** Two divergent copies of the pair model, chat, document, PDF, vault within a month. |
| Import across tools (`/en-gb/narrated-review/api/…`) | Rejected. Tools are independently versioned deployables; one tool must not depend on another tool's URL. |
| **Extract to a shared core module, both tools pin it** | **Recommended.** The third extraction in this family, after `core/sg-zip` and `core/sg-live-capture`. `narrated-review` re-pins with no behaviour change; `video-review` consumes it from day one. |

Proposed: **`core/sg-review-pairs` v0.1.0** — the pair/session model, structural
editing, document assembler, chat (both scopes) + its transport, PDF, zip and
vault writer. What stays tool-local is only what differs: how pairs get *made*.

## Decision 7 — Frames are extracted with what already exists

`core/sg-video-frames` v0.1.0 `extractFirstFrame(file, { at })` seeks to an
arbitrary timestamp — the name undersells it (worth adding an `extractFrameAt`
alias in the same version family). Fast path is a native `<video>` seek + canvas
draw; the fallback is FFmpeg `-ss <at> -frames:v 1` for codecs the element
cannot decode (HEVC `.mov` on most desktops). Both re-encode through a canvas,
so **no source metadata survives** — the same privacy guarantee the HEIC tool
relies on.

For candidate sampling, prefer **one `<video>` element seeked repeatedly** over
one call per frame: seeking a decoded element is far cheaper than re-opening the
file, and a 20-minute video with 40 pairs × 8 candidates is 320 seeks.

## Decision 8 — Privacy and cost, stated as plainly as the sibling tool

- The video **never leaves the browser**. What leaves, and only to OpenRouter
  under the user's own key: the **audio clips** (transcription) and, when
  screenshot-grounded cleanup or model-refine is on, **the chosen frames**.
- The same `cleanup: 'grounded' | 'text-only' | 'off'` control applies, and the
  same spend cap covers transcription, cleanup, refine and chat.
- **Cost shape differs from the sibling tool.** There, a session is minutes long
  and human-paced. Here a one-hour video could produce a hundred segments before
  grouping. Defaults must therefore be: grouping **on**, model-refine **off**,
  and a cost estimate shown *before* processing starts, with the spend cap
  offered in the same breath.

## Decision summary cheatsheet

| # | Decision | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Timestamps | Cut first with VAD, then transcribe — bounds are structural, never model-claimed. Local Whisper is the opt-in word-level lane |
| 2 | Audio extraction | The publisher's route ladder (FFmpeg remux → decode-to-WAV) |
| 3 | Frame timing | The picture leads the words; search a window with a lead AND a lag, never the midpoint |
| 4 | Frame choice | Settle detection over sampled candidates; keep all candidates for one-click switching; model-refine is opt-in |
| 5 | Grouping | Merge adjacent segments sharing a frame; reversible; the main cost lever |
| 6 | Sharing | Extract `core/sg-review-pairs`; both tools pin it; no tool-to-tool imports |
| 7 | Frame extraction | `core/sg-video-frames` with `{at}`, one seeked element for candidates |
| 8 | Privacy & cost | Video stays local; estimate before processing; grouping on, refine off by default |
