# Narrated Review: You Are Not Recording A Video Badly, You Are Authoring A Document Cheaply, And The Keypress Is Doing Three Jobs

**version** v0.33.59
**date** 16 August 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Engineering, Product

**type** Architecture brief

*Second of 16 August, covering two memos describing one tool. Contains one practical catch that would otherwise be found in testing, and one argument grounded in this corpus's own transcription record. Offered to be built on and challenged.*

---

## What This Is

A capture tool that turns a narrated walk through a screen into an artefact a language model can read directly, and the observation that makes the design obvious: **the memo asks why a whole video is needed when the point is a few screenshots, and the answer is that none is, but the sharper framing is that this is not a worse video, it is a cheaper way to author a structured document, since the expensive parts of making a model understand a screen recording are choosing which frames matter and pairing each with the words about it, and a person pressing a key while narrating does both at capture time for free; that keypress is doing three jobs rather than the one the memo notices, because it marks a screenshot, bounds an audio segment that can be transcribed independently and in parallel, and creates the alignment between an image and the words about it, and the third is the whole product since a video has no such binding and a transcript alone has no images; one practical catch will otherwise surface in testing, because people begin speaking before they press and continue after, so audio must be captured continuously with the keypress as a marker rather than as a start and stop control, or the first second of every segment is lost; the proposal to send the current screenshot to the model doing the transcript cleanup is the strongest idea across both memos and this corpus has direct evidence for it, since a text-only corrector on noisy audio has been measured roughly doubling the error rate while improvement came from supplying information beyond the text, and these very memos contain the proof, having rendered the product's own name three different wrong ways in transcripts where a screenshot of the page under discussion would have shown the correct spelling; the raw transcript must survive alongside the cleaned one, which is the same source-and-derived discipline applied everywhere else in the corpus; and the acceptance test is stated in the memo itself, that pressing send should require no cleanup afterwards, which is a testable criterion rather than an aspiration.** It is the second document of 16 August (cross-ref: the v0.33.58 in-page chat brief, the v0.33.56 workflows brief, the v0.33.55 vaults-as-substrate brief, the v0.33.58 site changes brief, and the v0.33.59 supplement vault brief). New contributions: **the reframe from recording to authoring, the keypress doing three jobs with alignment as the product, the continuous-buffer catch, the corpus evidence for screenshot-grounded cleanup, and the output format decided by who reads it.**

## The Reframe: Authoring, Not Recording

The memo's own objection is the right starting point. The project lead: **"why the hell do I need to send a full video if I just want to share some screenshots of it?"**

Correct, and the reason is worth stating precisely because it explains the whole design.

Making a model understand a screen recording requires two expensive things: **deciding which frames matter**, and **pairing each frame with the words that refer to it**. Sampling frames from a video loses the pairing; keeping the whole video is costly and mostly redundant, since a screen recording is largely identical frames.

**A person narrating while pressing a key does both of those at capture time, for nothing.** They select the frame by pressing at the moment it matters, and they bind it to the narration by talking about it immediately after.

So the tool is not producing a degraded recording. It is producing **a structured document**, and the capture method is simply the cheapest way to author one. That reframe matters because it decides the output format: the target is a document, and the video-like capture is incidental.

## The Keypress Does Three Jobs

The memo notices one of them. The project lead: **"this probably would also allow transcription to be done in bits, because every time I click on it or press a key, I am basically at a different section, so we can send that audio already for the transcript."**

That is true and it is the least important of the three.

| Job | What it gives |
|---|---|
| Marks a screenshot | The frame worth keeping, chosen by the only party who knows |
| Bounds an audio segment | Short clips transcribe faster, in parallel, and more accurately than one long stream |
| **Creates the alignment** | **This image, and these words about it** |

**The third is the product.** An artefact where each image is bound to the sentences about it is directly consumable by a model, and neither of the alternatives has it: a video contains the alignment implicitly and nobody can extract it cheaply, and an audio transcript has no images at all.

Worth saying because it should drive the data model. The unit is not a screenshot and not a transcript segment. **The unit is a pair**, and everything downstream operates on an ordered list of pairs.

## One Practical Catch

Something that will otherwise be discovered in the first real session.

**People start talking before they press, and keep talking after.** The natural rhythm is to see something, begin describing it, and press the key partway through the sentence. A design where the keypress starts and stops recording loses the beginning of nearly every segment.

So: **capture audio continuously, and treat the keypress as a marker rather than a switch.** A ring buffer with a few seconds of lookback means the segment can begin before the press. And because boundaries are markers over a continuous recording rather than separate files, they can be **adjusted in review** by dragging, which the memo's editing pass will want anyway.

The screenshot is the opposite case and is simple: capture it at the instant of the press, since that is the state being pointed at.

## Screenshot-Grounded Cleanup Is The Strongest Idea Here

The second memo proposes it almost in passing and it deserves to be a headline feature. The project lead: **"if we send to the other LLM the screenshot of what we are currently talking about, and maybe even the summary, so that the other LLM has more context."**

**This corpus has direct evidence that it works, and direct evidence of the failure it prevents.**

The evidence that it works: the transcription brief of 6 August established that a **text-only** model correcting recogniser output on a noisy set raised the word error rate from around thirty-one percent to around sixty-five percent, roughly doubling it, and that improvement came from supplying the correcting model with information beyond the text.

**A screenshot is exactly that information**, and for screen narration it is unusually good, because the words most likely to be misheard are the ones written on the screen being discussed.

The evidence of the failure is in these two memos themselves. Across this session's transcripts, the product's own name has appeared as **SGCN**, **Eskit** and **Skid**; a well-known author became an unrelated pair of words; a high-street retailer became a phrase about a barracks; and a widely known acronym became something else. **Every one of those would have been correctable from a screenshot of what was on screen at the time.**

So the cleanup step should receive, per segment: the raw transcript, the screenshot, and the rolling summary. That last one supplies vocabulary established earlier in the session, which is what fixes a term the first time it appears and keeps it fixed.

**And the cleanup should mark rather than silently resolve**, following the same discipline the corpus adopted for two-model transcription: where the model is unsure, surface it for the review pass rather than choosing confidently.

## Keep The Raw Transcript

The memo describes both views and both should persist. The project lead: **"you can have the raw transcript, which tends to be a stream of consciousness, and then you can have the voice, the description."**

The rule the corpus applies everywhere: **the derived artefact is useful and the source stays available.** A cleaned transcript is what gets sent; the raw one is what settles a dispute about whether something was said or invented. It costs almost nothing to keep and it is unrecoverable once discarded.

## The Rolling Summary, And What Not To Send

The memo has the cost shape right. The project lead: **"we're not going to send everything, but we can just send some information, or maybe the summary so far."**

That is the correct instinct and worth making explicit as a rule, because the naive version gets expensive quickly:

| Sent per segment | Cost | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| All previous screenshots | Grows quadratically | No |
| All previous transcript | Grows linearly, quickly large | No |
| **Current screenshot plus rolling summary** | Roughly constant | **Yes** |

The rolling summary is itself a derived artefact updated after each segment, which makes it another instance of the same pattern: cheap to maintain incrementally, expensive to reconstruct from scratch.

## Annotation: Vector To Edit, Flattened To Send

The memo is undecided and both halves of its instinct are right. The project lead: **"if I can have some kind of drawing tools where I can do some circle, or some arrows, which actually are probably easier to record because you can probably record it as vector graphics."** And the fallback. The project lead: **"or just take a screenshot at the end, that works too."**

The resolution is the same source-and-derived split as everything above. **Keep the annotation as vector data**, because that is what allows it to be moved, removed or adjusted in the review pass. **Flatten it into the image for the output**, because the consumer is a model that sees pixels and will not interpret a separate overlay layer.

So each pair carries three things: the clean screenshot, the annotation data, and the flattened composite that ships.

## The Output Format Is Decided By Who Reads It

The memo lists options without choosing. The project lead: **"this could be done in a vault, this could be done as an SG Send file, this could be done as sending a message, putting up a zip file."**

The deciding question is what the receiving model can actually reach, and this corpus recorded the constraint on 14 August: many agents can only fetch URLs a search has already returned, so a link is not reliably followable.

| Form | Reaches a model? | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Markdown with images** | **Always** | The universal case, and the primary format |
| A zip upload | Always, if uploaded | Handing to any assistant |
| A vault with a read key | Only if it can fetch | Sharing with a person, or an agent you control |
| A message to a vault | Not directly | Filing into a workflow |

**So the primary artefact should be a single markdown document with the images alongside it**, ordered, with each pair as a heading, an image and its text. That is what every assistant handles, it is human-readable, it diffs, and it needs no tooling.

The vault is then the **home** rather than the format: it holds the document, the images, the raw transcripts and the annotation data, and gives the memory the memo wants. The zip is the export for handing to an assistant that cannot fetch.

Naming note: the memo says SG Send file, which the transcript may have garbled. Worth not inventing a new container format when a folder in a vault plus a markdown file already does the job.

## Why The Vault Is The Right Home

The memo reaches this and the reasons are good. The project lead: **"the advantage of putting this in a vault, I think we should put it in the vault, because it's a good workflow for vaults, and it makes it easy. The only thing I need to do is share the vault key."**

Three properties beyond convenience.

**Memory across sessions.** The project lead: **"with memory, because you can then start to capture what you've done and what you haven't done."** A second review of the same vault can see the first, which means the tool accumulates rather than producing disconnected artefacts.

**Context lives with the review.** Notes about the situation, prior feedback and what changed since can sit alongside, so the model reading it gets the history rather than a snapshot.

**And it is a genuine case study.** A feedback tool that lives in a vault, produces a shareable artefact and is shared by handing over a key exercises the whole stack on a real workflow, which is the same argument that makes the other demonstrations good.

## What Exists And What Is New

The memo's estimate is plausible and worth breaking down, because the new parts are small and specific.

**Already built:** screen and video capture, voice recording, transcription, chat with vault contents, vault packaging and sharing, markdown rendering, and the tools deployment that hosts this class of thing.

**Genuinely new:**

- The **segmentation loop**: keypress, screenshot, marker into a continuous buffer.
- The **pair data structure**, and everything operating on an ordered list of pairs.
- The **review and edit pass**: image and text side by side, adjustable boundaries, editable cleanup.
- The **cleanup call** that sends screenshot plus rolling summary alongside the raw text.
- The **document assembler** producing ordered markdown.

That is a small amount of new code over a large amount of existing capability, which matches the memo's estimate.

## The Acceptance Test

Stated in the memo and worth adopting literally. The project lead: **"in the end, I want to just be able to press send and not worry about it. You don't want to have the context load of creating an analysis and then spending time cleaning it up to give to an LLM."**

That is testable: **if a session requires editing before it can be sent, the tool has not worked.** The review pass should be optional polish rather than required repair, and the first real session should be measured against that rather than against whether the features exist.

It is also the same standard the corpus has applied to every capture tool this month, where the value was located in removing a context switch rather than in adding a capability.

## Build Order

1. **The segmentation loop and the pair structure.** Keypress, screenshot, continuous audio with markers. Nothing else works without it.
2. **Per-segment transcription**, in parallel, since segments are independent.
3. **The markdown assembler**, producing a sendable document. At this point the tool is useful even with no cleanup and no review.
4. **Screenshot-grounded cleanup**, with the rolling summary.
5. **The review pass**, with adjustable boundaries and editable text.
6. **Annotation**, vector kept and flattened for output.
7. **The screenshot-only variation** the memo mentions as a second mode, once the first is working.

Step three is the milestone worth aiming at, because a plain ordered document of screenshots and raw transcripts is already better than a video and can be tested against the acceptance criterion immediately.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a screen recorder.** A document authoring tool whose input happens to be narration.
- **Not one long transcript.** An ordered list of image and text pairs, which is the alignment.
- **Not a start-stop control.** Continuous capture with the keypress as a marker.
- **Not a text-only cleanup.** The screenshot is what fixes the words that are on the screen.
- **Not a new file format.** Markdown and images in a vault, with a zip for export.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Keypress segmentation | It gives alignment for free and imposes a rhythm on the person narrating, which may feel unnatural at first |
| Continuous capture | It solves the lost-first-second problem and means audio is always recording during a session |
| Screenshot in the cleanup call | It fixes exactly the errors that matter and adds an image to every transcription request, which has a cost |
| Keeping raw and cleaned | Provenance is worth it and it doubles the text stored per session |
| Markdown as the primary format | Universally readable and it loses any structure a richer format could carry |
| Press send with no cleanup | It is the right bar and it means the cleanup step has to be good enough to trust unreviewed |

## Open Questions

| Question | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| What is the lookback window? | A few seconds, tuned against how people actually narrate |
| Is the keypress the only boundary? | A pause detector could suggest boundaries the user did not mark |
| How is the rolling summary maintained? | Updated after each segment, and what happens when it grows long |
| Where does the annotation vector format come from? | Existing tooling if possible, since this is the least novel part |
| Does the model see annotations as a separate signal? | The flattened image shows them; whether their coordinates also help is worth testing |
| How is a session named and found later? | Memory across sessions needs an index, which is the catalogue question again |
| Screenshot-only mode: same pipeline? | It should be, with a different capture front end |
